## The Beginning

My journey into computer vision started during my undergraduate studies at IIT Jodhpur. I was fascinated by how machines could "see" and understand the world around them.

## Early Projects

### Face Recognition

One of my first projects involved face recognition systems. Working with Prof. Richa Singh, I learned about:

- Biometric systems
- Deep learning for face verification
- Identity leakage in generative models

This experience led to my work on SynthProv, which analyzes identity signals in StyleGAN-generated images.

### Quantum Computing Meets Vision

At MPI-INF in Germany, I explored an unusual intersection: quantum computing and computer vision. This led to CCuantuMM, our CVPR paper on quantum-hybrid shape matching.

Key learnings:
- Quantum annealing for optimization
- 3D shape matching
- Cycle consistency in multi-shape problems

## Industry Experience

### Avataar.ai

Working as a Research Engineer at Avataar.ai for two years was transformative. I got to:

1. **Build Production Systems**: Moving from research prototypes to production-ready code
2. **Scale Matters**: Learning about inference optimization and real-time constraints
3. **3D Reconstruction**: Working on neural rendering and 3D generation

The experience taught me that shipping working products is very different from publishing papers.

## Graduate School

Now at CMU pursuing my Master's in Computer Vision, I'm diving deeper into:

- Generative models (diffusion models, flow matching)
- Neural rendering (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting)
- World models and scene understanding

## Key Takeaways

After several years in computer vision research, here's what I've learned:

1. **Start Simple**: Begin with basic implementations before adding complexity
2. **Read Papers Deeply**: Don't just skim - implement key ideas
3. **Collaborate**: Working with others exposes you to new perspectives
4. **Ship Code**: Research without code is just ideas
5. **Stay Curious**: The field moves fast - keep learning

## What's Next?

## Resources for Beginners

If you're starting out in computer vision:

- **Courses**: CS231n (Stanford), 16-385 (CMU)
- **Papers**: Start with foundational work (AlexNet, ResNet)
- **Code**: Implement papers from scratch
- **Community**: Join Discord/Slack groups, attend conferences

The journey is challenging but incredibly rewarding. Keep building! 🚀
